# PulseTrackr — Marketing

> Community safety radar for where you actually are. This file holds App Store
> copy, positioning, and the pitch narrative. Keep claims honest: PulseTrackr does
> **not** contact emergency services — SOS notifies user-selected trusted contacts only.

---

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** A community safety radar — see, report, and verify what's happening
near you, and send SOS to the people you trust.

**The wedge:** Everyone can collect reports. The hard, valuable problem is making
them *believable*. PulseTrackr is built as a trust layer — server-authoritative
verification, dispute mechanics, confidence tiers, abuse rate-limiting — so local
rumors either earn confidence fast or die fast.

**Principles we market on:**
- **Proximity, not surveillance** — you see your watch radius, not the whole world.
- **Privacy by architecture** — public pins are offset from the reporter's exact
  spot; trusted contacts stay on-device; no advertising trackers.
- **Honest scope** — we don't pretend to dispatch police. Trust compounds;
  overpromising destroys it.

### Description

**Safety starts with knowing.**

PulseTrackr is a community safety radar for the place you actually are. Instead of a
global feed of distant alarms, you see what's happening *near you* — a robbery two
streets over, a flooded road on your route, a market fire, gridlock building before
you walk into it. Reported by neighbors. Verified by neighbors. Quiet until it
matters.

**See what's near, not what's noise.**
Incidents appear within your watch radius — 3 km by default, up to 15 km. No
doomscrolling the whole world. Just the live local context that helps you decide
which way to walk, drive, or wait.

**Reports you can trust.**
Every report earns its confidence. Neighbors confirm or dispute what they see, and
the most useful signal — *is this still happening?* — rises to the top. Verified,
multiply-reported, and official updates are clearly marked, so you know what's a
rumor and what's real.

**SOS, on your terms.**
Hold for SOS and PulseTrackr quietly streams your location and direction of travel to
the trusted contacts *you* chose — with battery and movement context so they can
actually help. Your contacts stay private, stored only on your device.

**Privacy built in, not bolted on.**
- Public incident locations are offset from the reporter's exact spot.
- Your trusted contacts never leave your device.
- Report with no location at all if you prefer.
- No advertising trackers.

**Honest about what it is.**
PulseTrackr does not contact police or emergency services. SOS notifies the trusted
people you select — no false promises, no faceless dispatch.

Built for communities where local safety information is scarce — and useful anywhere
people look out for each other.

**Download PulseTrackr. Move with context.**

## 3. Pitch Narrative

*Ten beats. The story, not the spreadsheet.*

1. **Title** — PulseTrackr — *Move with context.* The community safety radar for
   where you actually are.
2. **Problem** — In much of the world, the information that keeps you safe doesn't
   exist in usable form. You find out by rumor, or the hard way.
3. **Why now** — Smartphone density has reached the neighborhoods that need this
   most. People already crowd-report safety to group chats — chaotically,
   unverifiably, with no map and no memory. The behavior exists; the infrastructure
   doesn't.
4. **Product** — A proximity-bounded live map of local incidents (security, fire,
   traffic, weather, utilities, medical, structural). Report, verify, and consent-
   first SOS to your own trusted circle.
5. **Insight (the wedge)** — Everyone can collect reports. The hard problem is making
   them believable. PulseTrackr is a trust layer.
6. **Why we're different** — Proximity, not surveillance. Privacy by architecture.
   Honest scope. Adversarial engineering — the client can't forge "verified."
7. **Beachhead** — Start where the pain is sharpest: cities with thin official safety
   information and high mobile density. Already tuned for one such market (local
   language, local incident types) — built for a real place, not a whiteboard.
8. **How it grows** — Win one city's trust completely → density makes the map
   indispensable → expand city by city. Then layer official-source ingestion and
   reputation-weighted reporters.
9. **Bigger vision** — The trusted layer for local truth and consented personal
   safety: the thing you check before you leave the house, and the quiet protocol
   that has your back if something goes wrong, on terms you set.
10. **Where we are** — Credible 1.0: full test coverage, privacy manifest, abuse
    hardening, moderation, App Store–ready. Built like infrastructure. Ready for a
    focused first city.

## 4. Messaging Guardrails

Do **not** claim, imply, or A/B-test any of the following:
- That PulseTrackr contacts police, ambulance, fire, or any emergency service.
- That SOS guarantees delivery or response.
- That public incident pins show a reporter's exact location.

These aren't legal hedges — they're the brand. Trust is the product; an overpromise
that fails once is unrecoverable.
